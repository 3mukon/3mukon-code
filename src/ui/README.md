## Responsibilities

- Design tokens (`renderer/tokens.css`): the colours, an e-ink look after the Ink Paper theme on its Flexoki palette, switched by macOS's appearance: near-black ink on a warm grey paper in the light theme, and pale ink on dark paper in the dark one, with colour kept for states, changes and the accent, cyan in the light theme and orange in the dark one as Ink Paper has them; `menu-line`, the dashed ink line around a sheet that opens over the window, such as a select's list, whose ink the scrollbars' thumbs take too, and the `paper` utility, which fills a sheet, or a control that covers the window, with the window's own paper, grain and all, without a shadow, as e-ink draws it; `bubble`, the darker paper that fills the messages, the user's and the agent's alike; `well` for paper set apart under a dashed line, such as a tool call's output, a diff and a code block; `paper-well` and `paper-bubble`, which fill with the well's and the messages' papers in the same grain, each on an opaque base of its own colour, so that a fill inside another, such as inline code in a quote, adds no tint; `placeholder` for what an empty field shows, fainter than the labels around it; `scrim` behind an image enlarged over the window; the syntax colours (`syntax-*`), Flexoki's for comments, keywords, functions, constants, strings, variables, numbers, language features and what is invalid; and the grain (`grain-image`, `grain-opacity`): `renderer/grain.svg`, a tile of faint noise with dark specks for the light paper, and `renderer/grain-pale.svg`, the same with pale specks for the dark one, which the app module lays over the window's background, behind its content, and the paper utilities restore on their fills at the same strength, since an opaque fill covers it (L-0009). The background-image tokens (`paper-grain`, `well-grain`, `bubble-grain`) combine the grain with a veil of each surface's own colour, and are also used by Markdown's inline code, CodeMirror and the terminal's composition box. Also the blinking of the ellipsis after Working (`animate-blink`), what a wait says only once it has lasted 300 ms (`animate-appear`), and the system fonts (SF Pro, SF Mono). Tailwind exposes them as classes such as `bg-frame`, `text-muted` and `border-line`; its default palette is removed, so every colour comes from a token.
- Presentational components, which take their data and text as props:
  - `StatusDot` (`renderer/status-dot.tsx`): a status (running, waiting, done or idle) as a coloured dot, which keeps its size in a line of text too.
  - `WaitingIndicator` (`renderer/waiting-indicator.tsx`): the count of sessions waiting on the user, with a waiting dot, as a button for the title bar; it renders nothing at zero.
  - `Pane` (`renderer/pane.tsx`): a pane with a header as tall as the title bar (status, title, a detail with its tooltip, a close button) above a body that its content lays out and scrolls itself, the detail taking only the room the title leaves, so that where both do not fit it is cut short first and the title only once the detail is gone; it takes focus as a whole, is marked current while it has focus, and is dimmed while another pane has it. A header can show something at its start, before the status and title, such as what lies beside the window buttons, and actions at its end, before the close button, such as a file's choice between its rendered view and its raw text. A pane that holds unsaved changes shows a dot in its close button until the pointer or the keyboard reaches it, as macOS's documents do, and VoiceOver hears it after the title. A header can lie in the title bar, moving the window from its empty part, and leave room for macOS's window buttons; the title and detail are a handle, whose text does not select, that gives its parent the presses by which it moves the pane with the pointer, and never starts a drag of the system's.
  - `Divider` (`renderer/divider.tsx`): the line between the two parts of a split, side by side or stacked, placed by its parent; the pointer drags it, or it takes focus and moves with the arrow keys along the split (Shift for larger steps), Home and End, keeping each part at least `minPanelWidth` wide or `minPanelHeight` tall while the space allows (`renderer/split.ts`).
  - `Select` (`renderer/select.tsx`): a compact choice of one option, the chosen option's text in small type, or its short text where it has one, with a chevron after it, which keeps its left edge whatever the text's width; its list, a sheet of the window's paper (`paper`) under a dashed line and without a shadow, opens just below it, from that edge, and marks the chosen option with the select's own checkmark. A select in Chromium's customizable appearance (`appearance: base-select`) lies over the text, invisible but for its focus ring and its list, so that the keyboard and VoiceOver handle it as any select; it takes the text's size, never its longest option's, its own invisible label clipped to it, so that it covers nothing beside the text.
  - `Segmented` (`renderer/segmented.tsx`): a choice of one among a few options under a label and a line of detail, as a row of them under a dashed line on the window's paper, the chosen one drawn in ink as a question's default answer is; a radio group, which Tab reaches at the chosen option and whose arrow keys, Home and End choose another.
  - `SearchField` (`renderer/search-field.tsx`): a field to search with, a magnifying glass, the query and, while there is one, a button that clears it, keeping the focus in the field; Escape clears the query and closes the field where it can close, as does leaving it empty. Compact, in small type over a dashed line, it sits in a heading's row without changing its height; otherwise it is a box under a dashed line as wide as its place.
  - `Menu` (`renderer/menu.tsx`): a menu at a point, a sheet of the window's paper under a dashed line as a select's list is drawn, which opens below and to the right of its point, or above and to the left where the window has no room, with the focus on its first item. The arrow keys, Home and End move between the items, as the pointer does, and a letter to the next item it begins; a click, Return or Space chooses one. It closes without a choice at Escape, at a press of the pointer or a turn of the wheel beside it, and as the window loses the focus or changes size, but not at the release of the press that asked for it, which may come once it shows: it is a manual popover that closes itself, not an automatic one (L-0002). VoiceOver hears a menu of items, a checked one as checked.
  - `Markdown` (`renderer/markdown.tsx`, ADR-0010, ADR-0018): text as Markdown, with GitHub's tables, task lists, strikethrough and footnotes, math set by KaTeX in any of the delimiters models write (`renderer/math-delimiters.ts`) and code highlighted by highlight.js in the syntax colours (`renderer/markdown.css`); raw HTML is left out and unsafe addresses dropped. Inline code has its own fill and inset; a block's `pre` supplies both, whether its code names a language or not. It takes how to draw an image, which is otherwise a link to its address, never fetched; how to follow a link that is neither a web link nor one within the text, such as a path to a file; and a document's heading sizes in place of the activity's, which keep the text's size. The sessions module renders messages with it and the files module rendered Markdown files.
  - `QuestionDialog` (`renderer/question.tsx`): a question over the window, a modal sheet of the window's paper under a dashed line, hung just below the title bar: its message in bold, its detail, and its answers in a row, the first at the right and any beyond the second apart at the left, as macOS lays out a sheet's buttons. The default answer is drawn in ink and has the focus, so that Return gives it, and Escape gives the cancel answer; until it is answered nothing else in the window can be reached. VoiceOver hears an alert, with its message and detail. Only a close while it shows gives the cancel answer: the close event its cleanup leaves, which can arrive after the dialog has opened again as development mode replays its effect, answers nothing (L-0010); its tests replay the effect with the browser's queued close event and with a synchronous one.
  - `TooltipHost` (`renderer/tooltip.tsx`, ADR-0021): the window's tooltips, drawn in the page from the `title` each control already carries, mounted once by whoever composes the window. The `title` of the element the pointer rests on for half a second (`TOOLTIP_DELAY_MS`), or of the element the keyboard focuses, shows as a sheet of the window's paper under a dashed line, in small type, below the element and centred on it, or above it near the window's foot, inside the window, and hidden from VoiceOver, which reads the element's own name; the next titled element within a moment shows its own at once. While the pointer is over an element, its title waits in `data-tooltip-title`, so that macOS's own tooltip does not show as well, and comes back as the pointer leaves; a title React sets meanwhile moves aside too and updates the tooltip. A press, the wheel, Escape, the window losing the focus or the element going hides it, and an element pressed shows none until the pointer leaves it. The tooltips show only while the host is `enabled`, which its composer gives from a setting that starts off; while they are off, the title of the element under the pointer still waits aside, so that no tooltip of macOS's shows in their stead.
- The prompts (`shared/prompts.ts`, ADR-0013): what the main process asks the user in a window, as plain data both processes share: a menu's entries (`MenuEntry`), items with an id and a label, checked or not, and separators, and a question (`Question`), its message, detail and answers, the first the main one, with its default and cancel answers, either one a `Prompt`; and `Prompts`, which shows them and resolves with the user's choice, which the app module implements and the feature modules' main-process code asks through, and which the two components above draw.
- Icons (`renderer/icons.tsx`): the Lucide icons the app uses (ADR-0007), as components that draw in the current text colour and are hidden from VoiceOver.

## Not responsible for

- Text: components receive their visible and accessible text already translated, from callers that use the i18n module.
- State and data, such as which sessions exist or wait: the feature modules own them and pass them in.
- The window's layout: the app module composes the shell.

## Interface

- `renderer/tokens.css`, imported once by the app module's stylesheet, and `renderer/grain.svg`, which that stylesheet lays over the window.
- The components above, the icons, `clampRatio`, `minPanelWidth` and `minPanelHeight`, each imported from its file in `renderer/`.
- For both processes: `Prompts`, `Prompt`, `MenuEntry`, `MenuItem`, `Question` and `itemIds` from `shared/prompts.ts`.

## Dependencies

- Uses React and Lucide (`lucide-react`), and for `Markdown` react-markdown with remark-gfm, remark-math, rehype-katex with KaTeX's stylesheet and fonts, and rehype-highlight; it imports no other module; the rule `ui-imports-no-module` in `.dependency-cruiser.js` enforces it.
- Every other module may import it, and takes its icons only from it: the rule `icons-only-through-ui` keeps `lucide-react` inside this module.

## Invariants

- Colours come only from the tokens; a new colour is a new token in both themes.
- Every interactive component can be reached and operated from the keyboard and is labelled for VoiceOver; decorative parts such as status dots are hidden from VoiceOver, and their meaning is given in words.
- Icons come only from Lucide, through `renderer/icons.tsx`; a new icon is a new export there and, like the others, is hidden from VoiceOver, so whatever shows it names it in words.
