## Responsibilities

- The layout (`renderer/layout.ts`): a tree whose leaves are panels and whose other nodes split their space in two, side by side or stacked, each giving its first part a share of it. `reducePanels` opens items, filling the empty area, beside a panel or in a panel's place, where an item takes the place as a panel of its own, so that nothing the old panel showed goes on in it; lets a panel go on showing something else, as a session page does its session once it starts it (`show`); closes a panel, whose space goes to the rest of its split and whose focus passes to the nearest panel there; moves a panel beside another or swaps the two; focuses a panel; and resizes a split. A panel shows a session page for a folder, a session the app has open, a file, a terminal by its id with the folder it started in, the archived sessions, the accounts or the settings (`PanelContent` in `shared/panels.ts`); the last three have no folder (`folderOf`).
- `usePanels` (`renderer/use-panels.ts`): the layout and the focused panel as React state, with what changes them, for the area itself and for whatever opens items in it.
- `PanelArea` (`renderer/panel-area.tsx`), the main area:
  - Each panel takes its box in the area (`arrange` in `renderer/geometry.ts`), and the panels stay in the page in the order they were opened, so that a split or a close never moves a panel's content elsewhere in the page and nothing in it starts over. Each split gets the ui module's divider.
  - Each panel is the ui module's pane, with a close button. What it shows comes from whoever composes the area (`PanelView`): its title, detail, status, unsaved mark, body and actions at its header's end. What is left out keeps the area's own: a session page or session goes by New session and a file by its name, each with its folder's name and the full path as the tooltip, and an empty body; a terminal goes by the title it is given, with its folder; the archived sessions, the accounts and the settings go by the titles they are given. The focused panel is marked current and the others dim.
  - A panel may ask before it closes (`confirmClose` in its `PanelView`), from its close button or ⌘W, and before a drop in its middle takes its place; it stays when the answer is no.
  - The area starts at the window's top edge: the headers of the panels at the top lie in the title bar and move the window from their empty part, and the header under macOS's window buttons, which lie over the area while the sidebar is hidden, leaves room for them, and shows what the composer gives to lie beside them (`besideWindowButtons`), just after them. While no panel is open, an empty strip there moves the window and shows it too.
  - Drops: a folder or file dragged from one of the app's trees or from the Finder, or a session dragged from the Sessions list (`DraggedItem` in `renderer/layout.ts`), fills the empty area; near a panel's edge, within a quarter of its width or height, it splits the panel and opens on that side; in its middle it takes the panel's place, as a panel of its own, which takes the keyboard focus where the old one had it. A highlight shows where. Several items open one beside the other. A file or a session may show in several panels, and an item dropped in the middle of the panel that shows it focuses that panel. Finder items come as a drag of the system's and reach the main process as the paths of their files, which come back as folders and files.
  - Dragging with the pointer: the app's own items and panels drag with the pointer's own events rather than a drag of the system's, whose copy badge Chromium shows on macOS as such a drag enters the page (L-0003). Whoever composes the area hands it the presses on the items of its lists (`dragItem` on `PanelAreaHandle`, with the label the ghost shows), and a panel's title and folder name are its own handle. A press with the primary button drags once the pointer has gone 4 pixels from it, so that a click stays a click: a layer over the window then holds the pointer, with the grabbing cursor, and a ghost of what is dragged follows it. Let go over the area, an item opens there as a drop does; a panel let go over another moves beside it near its edge, leaving its place as a close does, and in its middle the two swap places. The drag ends as the button goes up, and what it dropped opens or moves once the frame that ends it has been drawn, so that the cursor leaves the grabbing hand before the drop's own work, such as laying out panels that show long sessions, keeps the page busy (L-0008). A panel's body renders again only when the panel or the view of it changes, not with the area's own changes, such as each step of a drag and its end. Let go anywhere else, a panel on itself too, or given Escape, or as the window loses the focus, nothing moves; and the click that follows a drag is not the pressed element's. A moved panel keeps its id, so what it shows does not start over.
  - The keyboard, from anywhere in the window: ⌘D splits the focused panel to the right and ⇧⌘D downwards, opening a session page in its folder, which a panel without one leaves to the window; ⌘W closes the focused panel; ⌘1 to ⌘9 focus the panels in reading order; ⌥⌘ and an arrow focus the next panel that way, and ⇧⌥⌘ and an arrow swap the focused panel with it. The keyboard focus follows the focused panel. Dividers take focus with Tab and move with the arrow keys. While no panel is open, these keys are left to the window, so that ⌘W closes it.
- Dropped items (`main/items.ts`): `droppedItems` tells the folders from the files among the paths of items dropped from the Finder, a link counting as what it points to, and leaves out relative paths and anything missing or neither.
- The contract (`shared/panels.ts`): `PanelContent`, `DroppedItem`, and `PanelsBridge`, what the window asks of this module through the preload bridge.
- The presentation contract (`shared/presentation.ts`), by which the main process asks the window to present what an agent shows beside its session (ADR-0026, ADR-0027): what each of a presentation's panels shows, a browser tab, a computer target's preview, a file or an artifact main holds, with the artifacts' formats and limits; where it goes, beside its session's panel, or at that side of the area while the session has no panel open; the commands `present`, `update`, `arrange` and `close`, each carrying main's IDs for the command, the page's epoch, the session and the presentation, with the presentation's version as its agent last saw it; the window's answers, applied, refused with a code or cancelled, with the presentation's state: its version and where each of its open panels lies; its reports of a presentation's latest state after any change that no command for it made, such as the user's closes, moves and resizes, the layout around it changing or another presentation taking its place, and of the user's requests to see a presentation again, or a tab or application the session uses that no presentation shows; its snapshots, as a page starts and whenever the sessions' own panels change; and `PresentationBridge`, what the window asks of main through the preload bridge. A presentation never takes the keyboard focus, so no command asks for it.
- The window's rules for those commands (`PresentationLedger` in `shared/presentation-ledger.ts`), one ledger for each page, apart from the layout that carries them out, which it gives a plan for each command it applies and which gives it the boxes of each presentation's panels. What a presentation's panels show is its agent's, and which of them are open is the user's, who closes them, and `present`'s, which opens them all. Every change to a presentation, by a command, the user or the layout, raises its version by one, and the window refuses a command whose version is not the presentation's, giving its current state, so that nothing sent from an outdated picture of it applies and the agent tries again from where it stands; a `present` for a presentation the page does not have, as after a reload, applies and counts on from its version. A presentation has one panel for each of its items, in order, and shows each thing once. `update` changes what the panels show, one item for each panel, and the title, and opens or closes none, so a panel the user closed stays closed whatever it shows; only `present` changes how many items a presentation has. The page keeps a presentation whose panels the user or its agent closed, or that another presentation displaced, and goes on taking its commands. A repeated command ID gets its first answer again, during a drag too, and is refused as a conflict when it comes with other content. A command that arrives during a drag waits for the drop, for up to `PRESENTATION_HOLD_MS`, and is then refused as busy, except an update, which places no panel. A presentation opens beside its session's panel, or at that side of the area while the session has none open, and where there is no room it takes the place of that session's presentation presented last. Nothing closes, or shows anything else in, a panel that holds unsaved changes. The panel area does not carry out these commands yet.
- The fake window (`FakePresentationWindow` in `shared/presentation-fake.ts`), for tests of main's side: it answers by the same rules over an area it only pretends to lay out, and lets a test act as the user, who opens and closes sessions' panels, edits files, drags, and moves, resizes and closes panels; let time pass; or reload the page.

## Not responsible for

- What a panel shows: the app module composes it, from the sessions module's session pages and the files module's editors, which decide whether a panel may close.
- Reading files, or checking that a file lies inside a workspace or was dropped: the files module does when it opens one.
- The presses on the trees' and the Sessions list's rows: the workspaces and sessions modules hand them on, and the app module gives them to the area.
- Tabs inside a panel, and keeping the layout across a restart, which are deferred.
- Agents' requests and reuse keys, what an agent may present, the notices of presentations' changes for the agents and the panel list: main's side of the presentation contract keeps them.

## Interface

- For the app module: `PanelArea`, `PanelView`, `PanelAreaHandle` and `Press`, `usePanels` and `Panels`, `contentOf`, `panelsOf`, `Panel` and `DraggedItem` from `renderer/`; `droppedItems` from `main/items.ts`.
- For both processes: the types in `shared/panels.ts` and `shared/presentation.ts`; `PresentationLedger` from `shared/presentation-ledger.ts`, for the panel area; and `FakePresentationWindow` from `shared/presentation-fake.ts`, for tests of main's side.

## Dependencies

- Uses the ui and i18n modules, React and react-i18next, and in `main/` Node.js built-ins. The rule `panels-only-to-ui-and-i18n` in `.dependency-cruiser.js` enforces it.
- Only the app module imports it.

## Invariants

- A panel keeps its id while it is open, and the panels stay in the page in the order they were opened.
- Items reach panels by absolute path only, and sessions by the app's id with their folder's absolute path; an item from a tree is a row inside a workspace.
- The layout and its geometry have unit tests (`renderer/*.test.ts`); the area is rendered with a fake bridge, a panel that asks before it closes among its panels, and items and panels dragged with pointer events (`renderer/panel-area.test.tsx`); dropped items are checked on real folders (`main/items.test.ts`); and smoke tests drag tree rows and sessions into the built app's main area, work its panels from the keyboard and move them, starting no drag of the system's (`tests/smoke/panels.spec.ts`, `tests/smoke/sessions.spec.ts`).
- The presentation contract's limits and the window's rules have unit tests, the rules run through the fake window, with the plans they give the layout checked on their own (`shared/presentation.test.ts`, `shared/presentation-fake.test.ts`, `shared/presentation-ledger.test.ts`).
